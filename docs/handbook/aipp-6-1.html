OPERATOR CONSOLE
6.1.1

INTRODUCTION.

The console is the operator's eyes and hands: thirteen panels, the live game, the two grids, their verdict, and every control with a name derived from what it does. It drives the same bridge the agent drives (figure 6.1-1).

6.1.2

FUNCTIONAL DESCRIPTION.

The game image refetches five times a second while the world runs; the vision panel ticks its age every ten seconds without spending a model call; state arrives over server-sent events. A failure answers a 500 body instead of dropping the socket, so no panel ever blanks.

6.1.2.1

Honest Input.

Toggles read real booleans only: TRUE/FALSE/1/0/YES/NO/ON/OFF. Anything else leaves the state unchanged - a malformed request once switched the world on and blanked the video minutes later.

6.1.2.2

Naming Is Load-Bearing.

Seventeen controls carried no id until 2026-10-01, which is why the mobile tap-size defect stood for weeks: nothing could select them. The duplicate-id check must read the whole page, not only the controls - a button and a panel once both claimed vision.

6.1.2.3

Chat.

The chat box posts into the agent's own session. One probe used the wrong field name, read an empty-error reply, and nearly reported the wire broken. Verify the field before blaming the wire.
